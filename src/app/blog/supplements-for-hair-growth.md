## High-Evidence Supplements

### Iron (Ferritin Levels)

**Why it matters:** Iron deficiency is one of the most common causes of hair loss, especially in women. Your body needs iron to produce hemoglobin, which carries oxygen to follicles.

**Clinical consensus:** Low ferritin (<30-70 ng/mL depending on guidelines) correlates with diffuse shedding in both men and women.

**Dosage:** 18mg elemental iron daily with vitamin C for absorption (e.g., take with orange juice). Take away from calcium-rich foods which inhibit absorption.

**Expected results:** If ferritin was deficient, expect reduced shedding within 3-4 months of correcting levels. Visible regrowth may take 6+ months.

### Vitamin D3

**Why it matters:** Vitamin D receptors exist in hair follicles. Low levels (common in northern climates and darker-skinned individuals) correlate with alopecia areata and general thinning.

**Clinical consensus:** Multiple studies link vitamin D deficiency with increased shedding.

**Dosage:** 2000-5000 IU daily depending on baseline blood level. Take with fat-containing meal for optimal absorption.

### Zinc

**Why it matters:** Zinc supports keratin production and cell division in follicles. Deficiency leads to brittle, breakage-prone hair.

**Clinical consensus:** Effective only if actually deficient (check levels before supplementing). Excess zinc (>40mg) can cause copper deficiency paradoxically worsening hair health.

**Dosage:** 15-30mg daily from food or supplements. Zinc picolinate/citrate are better absorbed than zinc oxide.

## Moderate-Evidence Options

### Biotin (B7)

**The marketing myth vs reality:**
- Marketing claims: "Essential for strong hair!"
- Scientific reality: Only works if you're actually biotin-deficient (extremely rare, <5% of population)
- Common signs of deficiency: hair loss, brittle nails, rash — if you have these, biotin will help

**Bottom line:** Don't waste money on high-dose biotin unless your blood work confirms deficiency. The supplement is generally safe though ineffective for most.

### Collagen Peptides

**What the research shows:**
- May improve hair shaft thickness and reduce breakage
- Doesn't directly stimulate follicle growth
- Provides amino acids (glycine, proline) building blocks for keratin

**Dosage:** 10g daily with protein source. Works best alongside adequate overall protein intake.

### Saw Palmetto

**DHT blocking claim:**
- Plants contain phytosterols (beta-sitosterol) that weakly block DHT
- Much less effective than pharmaceutical finasteride
- Works best for early-stage thinning or maintenance

**Dosage:** 160-320mg daily of extract standardized to 85% fatty acids. Can take 3-6 months before seeing effects.

## Limited/No Evidence

### Mega-Dose Biotin Beyond Deficiency
If blood work shows adequate biotin (often 200+ ng/mL in healthy people), additional supplements provide no benefit but waste money.

### Proprietary "Hair Growth" Blends
Most contain:
- Small doses of established ingredients
- Ineffective herbal extracts
- Fillers and marketing fluff

Check labels for active ingredient names and dosages — be suspicious if brand name is the only thing highlighted.

## Supplement Timing & Stacking

### Morning vs Night

- **Morning:** Vitamin D3, B-complex (B vitamins support energy metabolism), zinc
- **With meals:** Fat-soluble vitamins ADEK, omega-3s
- **Night:** Magnesium for sleep improvement, vitamin C before bed if taking iron

### Avoid Taking With:
- Calcium supplements or dairy (inhibits mineral absorption)
- Coffee/tea within 1 hour of iron or B-complex
- High-protein meals right after biotin or zinc doses
